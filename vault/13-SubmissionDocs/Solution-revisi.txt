To close this gap, we built Lencana: a micro-course platform whose output is a credential that stands entirely on its own.

Lencana is not the authority; the institution is. Every course ships with a manifest carrying the institution's grading criteria, weights, and passing marks, and the hash of that policy is written into every issued credential—so a grade cannot be quietly re-explained after the fact, not by the school and not by us. Quizzes are graded by the publisher's server; an AI grading agent's essay score is only a proposal until a second reviewer, appointed by the publisher, approves it.

On the issuance layer, the institution's own agent signs attestations through delegation on the BNB Attestation Service (BAS), a public EAS deployment we do not own. The issuer never needs gas money: Lencana broadcasts and pays for the transactions.

Verification asks nothing of the checker. Issuer permission and credential status are read directly from BNB Chain: no account, no wallet, nothing of ours on the request path. Revocation is real, not implied: two bitstring status lists (revocation and suspension) are derived from chain state and re-checked against the chain on every read, and both list hashes are timestamped on-chain, so our own endpoint cannot quietly alter what a verifier reads.

Learners receive a soulbound artifact: one per credential, minted only while the credential is live, never transferable, never burnable.

Finally, the proof layer has a commercial shape. Machine callers buy verification in batches over HTTP 402; the payment is split on-chain, the publisher keeps the majority, and Lencana's share is a fixed percentage that can only ever be lowered. Live on BNB Chain testnet, with a demo issuer and its own test corpus.
